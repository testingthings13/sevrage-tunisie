Pregabalin (Lyrica) withdrawal: never a hard stop

Pregabalin — known by the name Lyrica — is a medicine diverted from its intended use. Stopping it causes a real withdrawal, and an abrupt stop at high doses can cause seizures: withdrawal rests on a planned taper, never on a hard stop. The protocol is decided after examination.

The signs of withdrawal

  • Intense anxiety, insomnia.
  • Sweating, pain, nausea.
  • Agitation.
  • Another diverted medicine, trihexyphenidyl, produces confusion and hallucinations, on stopping as in excess.
  • Stopping high-dose pregabalin abruptly can cause seizures. If life is in danger, dial 190 (SAMU), then call the clinic.

One of these signs settling in is enough to justify a call — and the call commits you to nothing. When in doubt, the test “What to do, right now?” points the way in thirty seconds.

The care at the clinic

For pregabalin, withdrawal rests on a taper planned over several weeks — never a hard stop —, treatment of anxiety and sleep, and a systematic search for the pain or the disorder that started the prescription.

It follows the same protocol as every withdrawal:

  • Admission assessment: clinical examination, assessment of how severe the dependence is and screening for associated disorders — anxiety, depression, sleep.
  • Medically supervised withdrawal, then stabilisation: the withdrawal syndrome is treated, not endured — tapering doses, monitoring, prevention of seizures; sleep falls back into place, mood lifts or reveals what it was hiding.
  • Work on craving: motivational interviewing, cognitive behavioural therapy, group sessions, a written plan for the tipping moments.
  • After discharge: close follow-up consultations, a family meeting, referral to a peer-support group — the first month outside is followed closely.

The detail, step by step, is on the home page: the protocol, from the first day to the last.

How long?

Allow 4 to 12 weeks of tapering, depending on the dose and how long use has gone on. Slowness is not failure, it is the protocol. These durations are indicative: they vary with how long use has gone on, the quantities, age and general health. The protocol is decided after examination.

For the family

You do not need the person’s consent to ask for advice: a first call serves to describe the situation, gauge how urgent it is and prepare what comes next. If he refuses to hear about it, the page “He refuses to come. What can you do?” details what to say, what to avoid, and when the emergency outweighs consent. If life is in danger — a seizure, confusion, loss of consciousness — dial 190 (SAMU) first, then call the clinic.

Two questions people don’t dare ask

Can pregabalin be stopped at home?

Never alone and never all at once: an abrupt stop at high doses can cause seizures. The decision is made after examination, never in advance — the taper is planned with the doctor, step by step.

Will it stay confidential?

Medical confidentiality covers the entire stay. No information is passed to an employer, an administration or any third party without the patient’s written consent.
