The detox cure, without the brochure words: what actually happens.

“Detox cure”, “rehab centre”, “detox centre”, “withdrawal”: the words change, the medical reality doesn’t. At Espoir Clinic, in El Menzah 9 in Tunis, a cure is a hospital admission: doctors, continuous monitoring, a protocol decided after examination — not a package holiday. Psychiatric emergencies are received 24/7 and a caregiver answers at any hour on +216 52 247 043.

What the word “cure” covers

A serious detox cure happens in three stages, and none of them can be skipped:

  • Medically supervised withdrawal: the acute phase, generally one week, under monitoring — because the body reacts to stopping, and for some substances that reaction can be dangerous.
  • Stabilisation: a stay of two to four weeks to restore sleep, treat what lies underneath the addiction — depression, anxiety — and begin the work on craving.
  • Follow-up: three to twelve months, as an outpatient, because that is where relapse is prevented.

Each substance has its own withdrawal, detailed page by page: alcohol · benzodiazepines and sleeping pills · opiates · cannabis · cocaine and stimulants · pregabalin · tobacco.

Cure, stay, trip: what to trust

Many search results for these words lead to intermediaries, not to places of care. Before entrusting someone to anyone — here or elsewhere — four questions are enough to sort things out:

  • Who is the doctor who decides the protocol, and when does the examination take place?
  • Who watches over the patient at night, and what happens if there is a complication?
  • Is the disorder that comes with the addiction — depression, anxiety — treated on site, or only the substance?
  • What comes after: who provides the follow-up, and how often?

At Espoir Clinic the answers fit in one sentence: a psychiatric clinic with nursing staff day and night, where supervised withdrawal and psychiatric care happen under the same roof.

The questions people don’t dare ask

Can withdrawal be done at home?

For tobacco or cannabis, often yes, with follow-up. For alcohol and benzodiazepines, stopping without medical supervision exposes the patient to seizures and to delirium tremens: the decision is made after examination, never in advance and never alone.

How long does inpatient withdrawal last?

The acute phase generally lasts one week. A stay of two to four weeks also makes it possible to stabilise sleep, treat the underlying disorder and begin the work on craving. The duration is reassessed every week with the patient.

Will it stay confidential?

Medical confidentiality covers the entire stay. No information is passed to an employer, an administration or any third party without the patient’s written consent.
